02 — WHERE THAT CURIOSITY HAS TAKEN MECURRENT WORK / 2026—

Right now,
at the surface.

A big ambition.
A specific question to work on.

My interest in fusion led me to look at the problems still standing in its way. Material erosion caught my attention: what happens to a surface when it has to withstand plasma, again and again?

That question brought me to plasma–material interactions. Today, I’m working towards an experimental system at Ankara University to study related interactions in an electric-propulsion context. It’s a way to investigate a question that began, for me, with fusion.

A QUESTION I’M WORKING ON

What happens when
plasma meets a wall?

Plasma–material interactionshBN

My current focus is hexagonal boron nitride (hBN), a material used in electric-propulsion systems. I’m interested in how its initial condition — including abrasion and small thermal cracks — may influence erosion under plasma exposure.

The work involves developing a literature-grounded experimental framework and exploring particle–surface models. The system is being developed to examine these interactions in an electric-propulsion setting, rather than in a fusion reactor.

Current stage: literature review and experimental design. The longer-term motivation remains understanding the challenges on the path to fusion energy.
